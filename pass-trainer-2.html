Why this works

Nobody holds 15 tickets by concentrating harder. They hold them by chunking and by having drilled the skill itself until the menu knowledge costs nothing.

Working memory is about four slots

You can hold roughly 3–4 items at once (Cowan). "Veggie fry, eggs poached, wg bread" is one chunk to a chef who knows the dish and three to one who doesn't. Menu Prep makes the dish knowledge automatic so your slots stay free for what's different about this ticket.

Recall beats re-reading

Pulling something out of memory strengthens it far more than looking again — the testing effect (Roediger & Karpicke). Every mode makes you rebuild, never just recognise.

Spacing, not cramming

Menu Prep uses Leitner boxes: nail a card and it returns in days, fumble it and it's back today. Spread reviews beat one long session (Cepeda et al.).

Interference is the real enemy

On a full rail, tickets blur into each other — that's proactive interference, not you being useless. The Stack trains it directly: tickets stay up like the real screen, land while you're still holding the last ones, and you call them back out of order — exactly what the pass does to you. The moment you turn to the stove, the ticket is gone from view, so the drill hides it while you rebuild.

Train just past your limit

Difficulty tracks your performance (deliberate practice, Ericsson). Read & Recall moves a level at a time; The Stack speeds up arrivals until you're properly in the weeds, because that edge is where the ceiling moves.

Ten minutes: 3 min Menu Prep → 5 min Read & Recall → one Stack service. Daily beats long.
Worth saying plainly: general "brain training" apps don't reliably make you better at anything except the app. What transfers is practising this exact task — read a ticket, hold it, call it back.

Install

  • AirDrop or email the file to yourself, or save it into Files.
  • Open it so it's showing in Safari. If it opens in the Files preview instead, tap share → Open in Safari.
  • Tap share → Add to Home Screen → Add.

Launch it from that icon: full screen, no signal needed. Your streak, levels and card boxes save on the phone.

If Add to Home Screen doesn't appear, Safari has the file open as a local preview and progress may not persist between sessions. The drills still work perfectly — you'd just be starting the levels fresh each time.
